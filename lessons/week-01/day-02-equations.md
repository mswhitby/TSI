## Mini-lesson — 7 minutes



An equation states that two expressions are equal. Keep them equal by applying the same operation to both sides.

Simplify each side, distribute when needed, collect variable terms, then use inverse operations to isolate the variable. Keep negative signs attached to their terms.

Substitute the result into the original equation. In a word problem, label the unknown and include any starting fee before the per-unit charge.



### Worked examples



**Solve 4x − 3 = 21.**



1. Add 3 to both sides: 4x = 24.

2. Divide both sides by 4: x = 6.

3. Check: 4(6) − 3 = 21.



**Solve 3(x + 2) = 2x + 11.**



1. Distribute: 3x + 6 = 2x + 11.

2. Subtract 2x and then 6: x = 5.

3. Check: 3(5 + 2) = 21 and 2(5) + 11 = 21.
