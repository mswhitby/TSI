## Mini-lesson — 7 minutes



A simple cost model is cost = starting fee + rate × quantity. Compare the same input and units for both plans.

To find when plans cost the same, set their expressions equal and solve. This is the intersection of two linear models.

The crossover tells you where costs are equal. To choose a cheaper plan for a particular situation, evaluate both models at that input.



### Worked examples



**Plan A costs $10 + $4h; Plan B costs $4 + $6h. At how many hours h are costs equal?**



1. Set 10 + 4h = 4 + 6h. Subtract 4h and then 4: 6 = 2h.

2. h = 3 hours. Both plans cost $22 at h = 3.

3. At 1 hour, A costs $14 and B costs $10; at 5 hours, A costs $30 and B costs $34.



**For A = 12 + 3h and B = 6 + 5h, compare the costs at h = 4.**



1. A = 12 + 3(4) = $24.

2. B = 6 + 5(4) = $26.

3. Plan A is $2 cheaper for 4 hours.
